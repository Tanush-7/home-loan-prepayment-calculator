How to actually use this

1. One extra EMI a year

Prepaying just one extra EMI every year (₹69,426 here) on an ₹80L loan cuts roughly 3–4 years off a 20-year tenure. Set it up for the month your bonus lands.

2. Always choose “reduce tenure”

Banks default to reducing your EMI because it keeps you paying interest longer. Put “reduce tenure, keep EMI same” in writing on the prepayment request.

3. Check charges first

Floating-rate home loans to individuals have no prepayment penalty (RBI rule). Fixed-rate loans can charge 2–3%. Confirm yours before paying a lump sum.

Does prepaying beat investing the same money?

Prepaying earns you a guaranteed, tax-free return equal to your loan rate — 8.5% here, with zero risk. An index fund may return more over 15–20 years but with volatility. A common middle path Bengaluru buyers use: keep a 6-month emergency fund, prepay until the loan is under ~50% of property value, then split between prepayment and investing.

When is prepayment not worth it?

If the lump sum would wipe out your emergency fund, if your loan is in its last 2–3 years (mostly principal left, little interest to save), or if you plan to sell the property within a year. Run those cases above — set the lump sum month late and watch the saving shrink.

How is the EMI calculated?

EMI = P × r × (1+r)n ÷ ((1+r)n − 1), where P is the loan amount, r is the monthly rate (annual rate ÷ 12), and n is the tenure in months. This calculator simulates every month, so lump sums and annual prepayments land in the exact month you choose.
